What you are looking at

Every frame is processed from scratch — nothing is tracked or carried between frames. The sensor is a Velodyne HDL-64E at 10 Hz; playback at 1× is real time.

The colour groups map directly onto the problem statement's categories. The ground is split three ways by drivability — drivable, marginal, non-drivable — from the slope, roughness and step of the same plane fit that found it. Static obstacle is any non-ground cluster the network classifies as background: walls, poles, vegetation. Car, Pedestrian and Cyclist are the dynamic-capable classes, drawn with their predicted 3D boxes.

filled terrain draws the ground as the sector wedges the classifier actually decides on, rather than as sampled points. The point view shows 5,000 of roughly 122,000 returns, so a perfectly coherent surface looks speckled — that sparseness is the sampling, not the model. Cells grow with range because the grid bins radius as √(r/R), the same foveation the output map is built on.

Terrain uses a muted good / warning / critical scale because it covers most of the scene and is context; the object classes stay saturated so they stay findable against it.

Switch to camera and toggle overlay. That view is the same prediction reprojected into the left colour camera through the calibrated velodyne-to-camera transform — not a redrawing of it. Turning the overlay off gives you the bare photograph, so you can check the classification against what is actually there rather than against a plot of itself. Road surface, kerb line, parked cars: they either line up or they do not.
Drivability is fused over time, not recomputed each frame. Computed per-frame it chattered badly — measured over 40 consecutive sweeps, 24.1% of cells changed class every 100 ms and 10.5% flipped drivable to not-drivable, which is a planner re-deciding one cell in ten at 10 Hz. Three changes fix it: a continuous cost instead of counting failed tests, a median over each cell's neighbours, and an exponential update across frames. Flip rate falls to 3.1% and 0.9% — a 12× reduction on the one that matters — and accuracy went up, because a lone disagreeing cell was usually a bad fit rather than a real feature.

A strong hazard still registers in a single frame: fusion slows marginal changes, not decisive ones.
Drivability is measured, not asserted. Scored against KITTI ROAD's annotations on 100 frames: F1 0.821, IoU 0.696 overall, and F1 0.889 with 0.934 recall inside 10 m — the range the problem statement wants at 5 cm resolution. It falls to F1 0.550 at 20–40 m, which is point density rather than a threshold: there is not enough evidence out there to fit a reliable surface. Cost: under 1 ms, no parameters, no training.
The greyed-out region is deliberate. KITTI only annotates inside the camera frustum, so the model was only ever trained on clusters within roughly ±40° of forward. Classifying the full 360° would be running it out of distribution and presenting the output as if it were trustworthy. Ground removal runs everywhere; clustering and classification run only where the model is valid, and everything else is drawn as unscored. Drivability is geometry, so it runs over the whole sweep regardless — only the object classes are frustum-limited.
"Dynamic" here means class, not motion. A parked car is coloured as a Car. Distinguishing moving from stationary requires comparing consecutive ego-motion-compensated sweeps — the residual-image approach from LiDAR-MOS — which this pipeline does not yet do. The data to build it is present: this drive has 108 consecutive frames with GPS/IMU poses.

The two estimators

grid_map is ANYbotics' filter chain, transcribed from NormalVectorsFilter.cpp and their demo config: a plane fitted by PCA over a circular neighbourhood, slope as acos(normal_z), and roughness as deviation from the local mean surface. ours takes slope as the largest step to a four-neighbour and roughness as the spread inside a single cell. Measured against SemanticKITTI road labels grid_map's wins at every operating point, at a matched 17.5% false-drivable budget, recall 84.7% against 65.7%, AUC 0.894 against 0.829, so it is the default. Switch between them: they disagree on about a third of cells, most of it ground we called marginal that grid_map calls drivable.

The controls

2.5D / 3D draws the same height field straight down, or projected and orbitable by dragging; 2.5D is the cheaper. height × exaggerates the vertical, the ground spans about 1 m of relief across 80 m, so at ×1 it is a plane. It scales the picture only, never a number. relief is hillshading from the gradient of the height field, so kerbs and the road crown catch light. objects draws static obstacles as pale columns, brightness by height above ground, and detections as boxes with a spur showing heading. elevation swaps the verdict colours for a hypsometric ramp over the 2nd–98th percentile.

What accumulation does and does not buy

Only returns inside the 20 m trust gate are ingested, because beyond it an observation is biased rather than merely noisy: the same patch disagrees between sweeps by a median 7.1 cm, ramping about 24 cm over 20 m of travel, which is a pose pitch error. Each sweep is therefore aligned in z against the map it overlaps. The consequence is that the map reaches much further behind the vehicle than in front, so ahead and behind are reported separately. The gain is dense memory of ground already driven, and distant terrain resolving properly once approached, not extra forward reach.

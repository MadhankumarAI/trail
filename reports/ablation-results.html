Why the 3D version fails

Before training anything, we measured whether PCA canonicalises consistently: show the same object to two sensors 90° apart, each seeing only the near surface, and compare the frames they produce. A canonicaliser that disagrees with itself between viewpoints has failed at its one job.

The prediction from that experiment showed up downstream exactly as expected.

Two of three objections were wrong. Sign ambiguity never occurred (0.0% of clusters) and eigenvalue degeneracy was rare (5.3%). Only partial observation mattered — and it mattered enough to make full-3D PCA worse than no canonicalisation at all.
Chart 3 · where detection is lost

Box tightness, not finding things

This diagnostic drove the next change. Centre error of 0.32 m is 8% of a car's length but roughly a quarter of a pedestrian's width — and the original loss treated those identically. Replacing it with a scale-relative corner loss plus binned heading cut heading error from 17.4° to 6.2°.

Chart 5 · a result that reversed a prediction

Precision problems are ranking problems

At loose IoU the pipeline was recovering only about two-thirds of what clustering made available, and the obvious suspect was volume: roughly 26 clusters per frame each emitting a detection, against about three real objects. The predicted fix was to reject clusters the classifier called background.

That prediction was wrong, and the correct fix was somewhere else entirely.

Why deletion fails and ranking works. Average precision integrates the whole precision–recall curve, so a false positive ranked below every true positive costs almost nothing. Deleting it removes a point from a harmless part of the curve — and a rejected cluster can never match a ground-truth box, so every rejection is a guaranteed miss whenever the classifier was wrong. Deletion has downside and no upside.

The ranking signal was the real defect. p(class) splits confidence across foreground classes: a cluster the model is certain is an object but is torn between Car and Van gets a mediocre Car score and sinks below genuine background. 1 − p(background) asks the question ranking actually needs — is this an object at all — and reordering the curve is precisely what AP measures.
